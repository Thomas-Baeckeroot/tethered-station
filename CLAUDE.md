Guidance for Claude Code when working in this repository.

## Overview

Personal research project: dynamics of a rotating, cable-connected space station for artificial gravity. It started as a generic study (three 1 600 t cylindrical modules, 0.38 g at 1 rpm) and converged on a concrete mission, the **retained solution**:

- Three SpaceX Starships, **vertical** (long axis radial, nose toward the spin centre), in a **triangle**, on the Earth→Mars Hohmann transfer (259 days). One crew ship, two cargo ships.
- Each ship is attached at the two Starship V3 catch points on the nosecone flanks, **13 m from the tip, ±4.8 m from the axis** (h = 21 m above the centre of mass; estimated from photos, notebook § 2.5; `tether_sim.ANCHOR_X`, `ANCHOR_SPREAD`, used by every campaign and linear model), each cable going to the neighbour on its side (bridle effect: restores roll). Internal reinforcement for the 30° side pull is assumed. Before 2026-11-01 the anchors were at 9 m from the tip, ±3.5 m (h = 25 m).
- Cables sized for full ships (1 600 t) at 1 g with safety factor 3. Since 2026-11-07 the reference is a **braided, sheathed, redundant HMPE cable** (SK99 grade, `--material sk99`, set in `run_scenarios.CABLE` and `deploy_sim` default): 136 cm² per side, 25.4 t for the three sides. The full ideal fibre (`--material dyneema`, 90.6 cm², 15 t, the `tether_sim` default and the generic-study cable) is the sensitivity case. In page text, name the generic material (« fibre HMPE », brands only as examples) and keep grade names only where numbers come from their datasheets. Dynamics computed at **610 t** per ship (mass after trans-Mars injection, 560–650 t range).
- Target gravity: Mars surface **3.711 m/s²** at **1 rpm** at the **main deck**, 14 m from the nose (`--g-ref-x 14`, R = 338.4 m there); the ships' centre of mass (34 m from the nose) turns at R = 358.4 m, cables 579.0 m unloaded (579.6 m loaded), 1.39 MN each. Before 2026-10-31 the target was at the centre of mass (deck at 3.49 m/s²). With `--g-ref-x`, felt tilt and gravity (`tilt`, `g`, `g_min`/`g_max`) are measured at the deck; `tilt_cm` keeps the rigid-part centre of mass value (≈ half), used for the Chrono comparison. Before 2026-11-06 all tilts were at the centre of mass.
- Propellant slosh modelled (Abramson pendulums), tanks with **baffles** (ζ = 3 %).
- **Tuned liquid damper**: 25 t water pool, Ø 7.8 m × 0.52 m, floor 12 m from the nose, tuned to 0.103 Hz, ζ = 0.15 (`tether_sim.POOL`). Cuts the worst out-of-plane sweep (to 0.4 Hz) from 6.8° to ≈ 0.74° at the deck; the residual peak is the main sway split by the pool (0.13 Hz), not the LOX mode (ζ = 0.30: 0.67° vs 0.74° in the simulator, wall wave 7 vs 12 cm, `T_zeta30_*`; the pool in the crew ship alone does most of the work, `T_bassin0_*`). The cable-stretch mode (0.53 Hz reference cable, 0.44 Hz ideal fibre) lies above the standard sweep band and is not damped: 1.6° at the deck in the in-plane sweep to 0.7 Hz (`T_balayage_t_07`), 1.7° (0.11 m/s²) under fixed-period forcing at 1.872 s (campaign `frequences`); it scales with the axial cable damping (ζ ÷4: 2.5° in the sweep, `Q_amort05_balayage_t_07`). Out of plane, fixed-period forcing also finds the cable string modes at 0.245 and 0.273 Hz (0.92° and 0.68° crew ship), which the sweeps barely show and the model hardly damps (cable damping is axial only). It is treated as a frequency to protect (source isolation, forbidden band ≈ 0.4–0.65 Hz, attachment damper in reserve; notebook § 6.4).

- **Cable-break safety** (notebook § 7), retained since 2026-11-01: **three third-section strands per side**, same cable mass (45.3 cm², Ø 7.6 cm each; `--side-strands 3`), plus cutters that drop a broken strand's pieces (`--release-delay 1`); campaign `brins3`, § 7.4, incl. a second break on the same side (`--break-cable2`, `--break-time2`; cable index = strand × 3 + side). One break: remaining two at 7.7 % of strength en route, 50 % in the design case (factor 2); two breaks: last strand at 15 % en route. The strands sit on a triangular plate on a ball joint at each nose fitting (equal sharing after a break, no mutual shading; not modelled, equivalent to a common fitting). Two half-section strands (campaign `brins`, § 7.5) were the earlier study: all strands of a side must leave from that side's fitting, since roll about the ship axis is held only by the bridle effect (`--strand-layout`). A hub (20 t) tied to each nose by two spokes (`--hub-mass-t 20 --hub-spokes 2`, campaigns `rupture`, `moyeu`, § 7.3) is the alternative that also covers the loss of a whole side or fitting (38 t): with it the station stays whole after a fitting loss, but the crew ship hangs from its other fitting with ≈ 14° floor tilt (`Z3_ferrure`); without it the ships tumble (`B3_ferrure`); it does not change comfort.
- **Deployment** (notebook § 5.4, `deploy_sim.py`, `deploy_campaign.py`), retained 2026-11-05: start at r = 60 m with 63 m of cable, 0.05 rpm, wait ≈ 3 h for the propellant to settle (estimate: 1 h would do at ζ = 3 %, the rest is margin), pay out at 0.03 m/s at constant ω (5 h), double ω to 0.1 rpm at full radius (1 h), then α = k·ω² to 1 rpm: 24.7 t in all, 8.2 t per ship, 6 % above the ideal. Spin-up duration changes neither the propellant nor the resonances (campaign `montee_lente`): floor tilt ∝ k, no amplification. Winch stall at 0.03 m/s: 26 kN peak (first shock; rebound peaks vary from run to run). Launch-and-brake fails: lateral thrust far above the axial acceleration.
- **Sun pointing** (notebook § 5.6, `sun_pointing.py`, campaign `soleil`): the spin axis follows the Sun (180° over the transfer) with out-of-plane pulses at the line of nodes (`--precess-rate`, `--precess-pulse`; 66–94 t hot-gas propellant). Fixed panels on the lit side; the heat-shield tiles stay in shade and radiate, so the propellant does not boil off (it needs ~1 kW of heating). A fixed axis would let sunlit tiles boil it off.
- **Other studies on top of the retained solution**: spin-up from 0.1 to 1 rpm with α = k·ω² (`--spin-from`, campaign `montee`, § 5.4); LOX baffles at ζ = 8 % or a second pool (campaign `lox`, `lox_search.py`, § 6.2): no gain at the deck; unequal masses after a propellant transfer (`--masses-t 220,805,805 --target-ship 0 --rpm 0.8621` with the catch-point anchors, campaign `masses`, § 5.5): the crew ship becomes ~7× more sensitive, and the crew walk (16 s round trip) hits its 0.06 Hz sway. Partial transfers and a low ballast (`--ballast-t/-x/-ship`, campaign `allege`, `light_tld.py`): its out-of-plane sway slows down and leaves the pool tuning; a retuned pool (Ø 8.8 m) brings half transfer, or full transfer + 50 t ballast, back to ≈ 0.9–1.2° at the deck at the worst rhythm. Sensitivity (campaign `sensibilite`, § 6.3): full V3 ships (693 t en route) and the full ideal fibre (`C_*`, `B3_dyneema_rupture`): same comfort up to 0.4 Hz; the stiffness only moves the stretch mode. With unequal masses, read the crew ship's values (`tilt_max_ship[0]`, added by `make_web_data.py`): the global `tilt_max` includes the cargo ships' static pivot.

- **Snap-back of a broken strand** (notebook § 7.4, campaign `entretoises`, `snapback.py`): once slack, the near half of a broken strand drifts outward and backward (Coriolis) and sweeps across the hull of the ship whose fitting is on the leading side, 3–4 s after the break, cutters or not. A boom (attachments at ±16 m) only delays it; a lanyard (`--release-keep 0`) makes it hang against the hull. Ties between the three strands of a side every 24–48 m (`--strand-ties N --tie-slack 0.1 --tie-k 2.6e7 --release-delay -1`, 48 segments) hold it: no contact, softer jolt; each tie carries about one strand tension. Peak values of the loose ends between ties depend on the discretisation.
- **Real load cases and creep** (notebook § 2.6): the 1 600 t at 1 g sizing case never occurs; it is kept because creep over 2 × 259 days and strand breaks give about the same section. Return ramped up to 1 g with 250 t ships (campaign `retour`, `--mass-t 250 --accel 9.81 --rpm 1.626`): same side tension as outbound (1.46 MN). Reference SK99 braid: 0.31 % creep at 20 °C over the round trip (cold cable: negligible, Arrhenius extrapolation unproven).

Rejected alternatives (keep as chapters, don't reopen unless asked): two ships (roll about the cable is neutral, drifts freely), horizontal ships (need masts, worse resonances), generic modules (preliminary study).

## Conventions

- The user writes comments, CLI help, UI strings and the pages in **French**; keep that for code and docs in this repo. Commit messages: compact one-line English, no model attribution, no Co-Authored-By trailer.
- **Commit as you go**: commit each coherent step (code change, campaign results, page update) without waiting to be asked. Remote `origin` = public GitHub repo; push only when the user asks.
- The Mac is often in use: run long computations at low priority (`nice -n 19`), few in parallel (`--jobs 2`), in the background. Several hours of compute are acceptable.
- `tether_sim.py` is the single source of the physics. `run_scenarios.py` holds the scenario catalogues (`CAMPAIGNS`). Keep both self-contained (NumPy + Matplotlib only) so they run in either venv.
- When changing a motion program or a diagnostic, re-run the affected campaign and `make_web_data.py`; the pages read everything from `docs/data/`.
- Both page footers end with « Dernière révision : YYYY-MM-DD » : update the date whenever a page's content changes.
- Page numbers quoted in prose must come from `docs/data/summary.json` (prefer `data-bind` spans) or from a script output; re-check them after any re-run.

## Physics pitfalls already met

- Rotating-frame relaxation: whole-system translation is unstable (centrifugal on an offset centre of mass); the centre of mass is re-centred every step.
- Explicit cable damping is unstable for realistic c/m; damping is applied after each step by an implicit solve over the whole chain (`Station.damp`: impulse unknowns per segment, tridiagonal per cable, Schur complement on the end segments; NumPy only). Before 2026-11-10 it was an exact exponential decay per segment pair, which under-damped the whole-cable stretch mode once c·dt/μ > 1 (55 % of the target rate with 16 segments, 19 % with 48): break recovery times were 2–4× too long and depended on step and segment count. `--zeta` now applies to a whole side (strands share c and k); before, each strand got it and a three-strand side was √3 more damped.
- Prescribed internal motions must have bounded acceleration: an instantaneous reversal shows up as a large horizontal jolt in the felt gravity.
- Slosh damping acts on the pendulum velocity **relative to the wall**, L(φ̇ − θ̇), not on the absolute angle.
- Felt floor tilt = ship tilt + lateral acceleration of the observation point / local g (θ + ü/g), at the deck (a_deck = a_c + α×r + ω×(ω×r)), not at the rigid centre of mass: the deck is 20 m above it and feels about twice the tilt. Sanity check: a free simple pendulum feels zero tilt. A sign error here once gave 0.69° instead of ≈ 2.7°.
- The structure is nearly undamped: steady-state frequency response is meaningless (81° peaks); use the time-domain sweep. The linear model, without cable damping, is conservative by 10–20 % against the simulator.
- Sweep curves (`sweep_response`, `tld_design.sweep`) must use **contiguous** time windows per frequency. Narrow ±10 s windows spaced ~135 s apart once missed the peaks (2.25° plotted vs 2.67° true).
- After a cable break without hub, ships tumble and cables would wrap around the noses: the model has no cable–hull contact, so only the first minutes are quantitative.
- Diagnostics that sum over all masses (L/I, centre of mass) break down once pieces are released and fly away; the spin rate and the diagnostic centre (distances to centre, roll) use the ships, internal masses, propellant and hub only, not the cable nodes.
- In shell heredocs that embed JavaScript, quote the delimiter (`<<'EOF'`) or write a script file: backticks and `${}` would otherwise be executed by the shell.
- The 3D viewer redraws cable chains at each event: the break (`anim.cut`) and the cutter release (`anim.release`). Without the latter, the cut attachment segments are still drawn and the released pieces look like they stretch back to the ships.
- `make_web_data.py` treats every `.json` in `out/<campaign>/` as a scenario: keep ad-hoc analysis outputs elsewhere (scratch dir).
- Fixed-period resonance scenarios (`RESO` 3.90 s for the LOX coupled mode, `RESO8`, `RESO_K`) must be retuned when the geometry changes: the LOX mode is lightly damped and very sharp (moving from 4.05 to 3.94 s dropped the free-slosh response from 3.0° to 1.8°). Scan the period with short runs before quoting a resonance.
- Diagnostics in `deploy_sim.py` must use tensions computed before the rest length advances in the same step: reading them after shows ṡ·dt·EA/L less (≈ 100 N at 0.03 m/s, whatever the cable length) and false slack. The `.npz`/`.json` `slack`, `Tmin`, `Tmax`, `Tcab` of runs from before 2026-11-05 carry this offset; `T` and the `t_detente_s` export do not.
- The standard sweeps stop at 0.4 Hz (`--sweep-f1 0.4`); the cable-stretch mode (≈ 0.5 Hz) needs `--sweep-f1 0.7`. Check the band against the modes (§ 12) before concluding that a peak is gone.
- `tilt_max` in summaries is the global maximum over the run; sweep-curve peaks are per-frequency maxima. Don't mix them when quoting a "peak at f Hz".
- Every run starts with the default kick of ship 0 (`--dv-radial 0.02 --dv-z 0.02 --droll 0.002`): it tilts ships 1 and 2 by ≈ 0.45–0.5° at the deck in the first seconds. For weak motions (crew walk, in-plane sweep, cargo with hub) the global `tilt_max` is that kick, not the motion: quote `tilt_max_ship[0]` (crew ship) for motion responses; keep the global value only for the perturbation itself and for breaks.
- Continuous sweeps underestimate narrow, lightly damped modes: fixed-period forcing (campaign `frequences`) found the stretch mode and an out-of-plane mode near 4.1 s higher than the sweep curves. Scan the period in small steps (0.005 s for the stretch mode, 0.01 s out of plane) and force long enough to reach steady state (≈ 6 min in plane, ≈ 10 min out of plane).
- Doubled hub spokes (`--hub-spokes 2`) leave from the fittings, one per fitting even with several strands per side. A fitting loss with hub is modelled with one cable per side (`Z3_ferrure`: side 0 and spoke 3 broken at the attachment): all strands of a fitting behave as one cable before the break.
